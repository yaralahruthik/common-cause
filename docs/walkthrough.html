Read this with the caveat. The sample of 15 food and beverage makers was chosen because their records are in the data, so it finds what it was chosen to find: 4 Hidden Concentrations and 1 tentative, affecting 7 of 15. A list of 15 companies drawn at random from private-fleet operators finds none. The tool also shows these three as two separate findings, a shared parent and a shared address. Joining them is still the analyst's job.

The problem, as the data allows it

Ownership is not supply

The goal was visibility past a buyer's direct suppliers, into who supplies them. None of the free sources records a single supply relationship.

GLEIF says

Who owns whom

Legal Entity Identifiers, and which company consolidates which in its accounts. 541,003 records in the slice, 259,753 Ownership Links.

FMCSA says

Who runs trucks, and where from

Every company with a USDOT number, private fleets included: status, fleet size, physical address, out-of-service orders. 192,915 Registrations with 10 or more power units.

Nobody says

Who buys from whom

Walking three GLEIF parent hops and calling the result "Tier 3 suppliers" would give a confident, plausible and wrong answer. So ownership hops are never called Tiers. The buyer brings their own direct relationships as a Portfolio, and the system finds the members that share a Common Cause: one Ultimate Parent, one physical address, one jurisdiction.

And "no parent on file" does not mean independent

Most companies file a reason in place of a parent. Of 383,816 US entities in the slice, only 1 in 20 names a parent. Some reasons really do mean "no parent": owned by natural persons, or not consolidated. Others only mean "not telling". Reading silence as independence would turn most of the graph into false reassurance, so every Entity carries one of three states, and every result says how much of it could not be verified.

Declared Parent 20,453 (5.3%) Declared Independent 197,584 (51.5%) Undisclosed Parent 165,779 (43.2%)

The two registries share no identifier: FMCSA carries no LEI, EIN or parent field. They are joined by name, address, phone and officer matching, and the join is thin. 2.1% of Registrations match a GLEIF record firmly, because most companies that run trucks have no LEI.

Architecture

A batch snapshot, a graph rebuilt in memory, three questions

No server to install and nothing to download: a clean clone starts from a committed Parquet snapshot. At start-up it is loaded into DuckDB and resolved in about 8 seconds. After that, a 25-name Portfolio is matched in about 40 ms and its exposure computed in about 60 ms. Dashed boxes are designed and not built.

  1. Source to storageOnly the slice that can be defended is kept: US companies plus every ownership chain they sit in, and every fleet of 10 or more trucks in any status, lapsed ones included. Values are stored exactly as published, each row with its As-Of Date.
  2. Storage modelSource Records are never edited. Matches between them carry evidence. Entities are groups of records joined by Firm Matches only. Ownership Links are walked to an Ultimate Parent, and each Entity's shareable causes are precomputed.
  3. Query pathMatch the names, let the analyst settle the uncertain ones, find the members that share a Common Cause, and show the evidence behind each Concentration.
Four decisions and what each cost

Each one closed a door

Find Hidden Concentrations, not Tiers

Against: a map of geographic risk; a continuous risk score per supplier.

Why
It is the question the data can answer honestly. A map would spend the time on geocoding. A score from one snapshot is a weighted sum that cannot be defended past the first "why".
Cost
No map, so a flood at one industrial park is not served. No single number per supplier. The answer is only as wide as the cross-registry join.
Would change
Historical census snapshots, which would make degradation over time measurable and a score defensible.

DuckDB and recursive SQL, not a graph database

Against: Neo4j; Postgres.

Why
There is one edge type to traverse and no chain is longer than 9 hops. The rest of the work is bulk loading and group-bys, which is analytics. A clean clone needs no server.
Cost
Every traversal is hand-written SQL, and there is a single writer. Cycles and broken chains are handled by hand: 16,287 chains break and fall back to the declared parent, and 2,436 walks disagree with it.
Would change
Real Supply Links, which bring variable-length paths across edge types. Or many users writing at once.

Hand-written matching rules, in three bands, never merged

Against: Splink's learned weights; embeddings; merging records into one clean copy.

Why
A Firm Match needs a strong name score and one independent signal: a ZIP code, a phone, an officer, or a pointer from a revoked number. Everything weaker is shown to the analyst with its evidence and never joins records. A rule I wrote can be defended; a learned weight trained on almost no true matches cannot.
Cost
Thresholds are hand-picked. Between the registries, Possible Matches are right only 33% of the time when corroborated, usually because they find a parent and its subsidiary at one address.
Would change
A few thousand labelled pairs, or analyst Verdicts piling up, at which point supervised weights win.

Count only what is firm, and say what is not

Against: counting every plausible match; reading a missing parent as independence.

Why
A risk tool that raises false alarms gets ignored, and one that falsely reassures is dangerous. A Concentration is tentative until two different Entities reach it through Firm or confirmed Matches. Unverifiable ownership is always counted and shown.
Cost
The headline is lower than the evidence may support: 7 of 15 affected in the sample, 9 if every Possible Match held. An analyst who skips the review queue sees less exposure than there is.
Would change
A Possible band accurate enough that counting a match is right more often than not.

How good is the matching?

102 Matches were sampled, 17 per band, and labelled. The labeller was an AI assistant, not a domain expert, so these figures measure agreement with one careful reader, not ground truth. 17 of 17 correct is consistent with a true precision as low as about 82%.

What was cut, and what does not work

Deliberately left out, and where the build falls short

  • LLM adjudication of the Possible band. Planned as the first extra and the first thing to cut, and it was cut. The precision table now argues for it.
  • Supply Links from USAspending. Federal subcontract awards are the one free source of real supply edges found. Designed and drawn; not built.
  • Delta ingest. GLEIF publishes every 8 hours, but a batch rebuild takes seconds at this size. The data is as fresh as the last manual run.
  • Open Supply Hub. Dropped: no access without a paid key, and a contributor link means "shared data about" a facility, not "buys from" it.
  • A graph explorer and a chat box. The questions are few and known. A generated query that is wrong fails where nobody can see it.
  • Linked Concentrations are not joined. The dairies above appear as two findings.
  • Industrial parks are discounted. An address shared by more than 10 names is treated as a registered agent's.
  • Small fleets are out. The slice keeps 4.3% of the census, and the reincarnating-carrier pattern lives mostly among small carriers.
  • Scale is argued, not tested. Blocking breaks first at fifty times the volume, and two blocks already exceed the cap today.
  • One user, no access control. Anyone who can reach the server can read or change any Portfolio.
